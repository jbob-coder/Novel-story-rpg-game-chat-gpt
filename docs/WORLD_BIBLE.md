# World Bible — Living Superhuman World (narration reference)

**Scope:** Universe foundation, not a completed encyclopaedia. This file separates established facts from *provisional scene-building material*. Read alongside `docs/CANON.md`, which takes priority for owner-approved lore.

## A. Confirmed world truths

- The story takes place on **Earth** and begins at **Day -30**, thirty in-world days before the first superhumans appear.
- Ordinary society and relationships matter before and after extraordinary powers emerge.
- At **Day 0**, a mysterious shockwave from a distant corner of the universe reaches Earth. Its true origin and nature are unknown.
- Human awakening follows a **hybrid** pattern: some people develop abilities immediately, some later, and others never. The shockwave also affects animals, plants and the environment.
- Heroes and villains are not preset species or classes; decisions and circumstances shape reputations.
- The world contains independently motivated individuals and institutions. Their actions do not require the player's participation.

Do not narrate the cosmic event as a known fact to civilians before they could discover it. No powers or wave-created ecological effects before Day 0.

## B. Civilian society baseline — contextual inference, not newly approved canon

At Day -30, treat Earth as a recognizable **ordinary society**: people need food, shelter, transport, health care, education, work, companionship and security. Their options vary by region and circumstance. The specific calendar year and global political geography are **not chosen**; the opening setting, Meridian Bay, is an original modern coastal city. Do not silently assume the laws of a real country or a fixed worldwide date.

The narrator may derive a local setting after the player selects (or implicitly establishes) a region. The following **functional roles** make a community coherent without requiring a locked city or cast:

| Social layer | What persists | Story consequences |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Household & personal network | trust, conflict, care, obligations, privacy | practical help, jealousy, loss, loyalty |
| School / work / livelihood | schedules, money, learning, status | skills, opportunities, discipline, missed commitments |
| Neighborhood / transport / services | routes, places, local risks, access | delays, chance encounters, shared crises |
| Local institutions | public services, employers, community organizations | decisions, scarcity, formal and informal authority |
| Regional and national structures | regulations, news, resource flows, travel | policies, economic shocks, official responses |
| Global society and environment | communications, ecosystems, scientific institutions | cross-border repercussions, Day 0 uncertainty |

The purpose is **causal links**: an NPC's shift, debt or family concern should affect their choices later. Do not use a list of institutions as a substitute for people or scenes.

## C. Story chronology — only fixed anchor is Day 0

| Phase | Confirmed | Narrative approach (proposed) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Day -30 opening | World is ordinary; no known superhumans | Establish a specific person, relationships, work/school or livelihood, place and immediate desire |
| Day -29 to -1 | First powers have not appeared | Let routine and independent local developments create stakes; no mandatory 30-scene countdown |
| Day 0 | Cosmic shockwave reaches Earth | Present sensory and social consequences through the player viewpoint, preserving uncertainty and uneven exposure |
| Day +1 onward | Powers can emerge later; ecology can change | Track discoveries, fear, cooperation, institutions, ecosystems and divergent NPC responses |

**Unconfirmed:** Whether telescopes or instruments detect the approach; warning time; how the wave looks, sounds or feels; its duration and physical effects; exact human, animal and plant transformations. Do not pre-narrate any as inevitable.

## D. Living-world causality

For each relevant world event, ask: who wants what, what do they know, what can they afford, what changes, who notices, and what survives to the next chapter? Track at least:

- **People:** identity, day/location, goals, commitments, relationships, expertise, health, resources, *knowledge* and secrets.
- **Organizations:** purpose, incentives, capacity, members, policies, competing interests and communication.
- **Places:** location identifier, environmental condition, connected travel routes and what inhabitants believe about safety or risk.
- **Information:** verified fact versus rumor; who discovered it, who relayed it, and when.
- **Consequences:** changing trust, skill, time, resources, reputation, access and future options.

Keep a detailed record only for consequential actors and locations; describe distant societal shifts at an appropriate summary scale. Relevant offscreen acts are resolved when in-game time advances through the story, not automatically between real-world messages.

## E. Possible tensions — **examples only, not occurred events or fixed NPCs**

A resident needing help conflicts with a player appointment; a classmate or coworker pursues a goal that competes with the player's; a store, transit service or employer faces an ordinary disruption; someone circulates a rumor that could affect relationships. These are templates for human-scale causes and consequences. None has occurred in the saved chapter.

After Day 0, possibilities broaden: a person hides a new ability; institutions disagree on risk; newly altered wildlife changes local travel; a plant transformation disrupts supplies; an unpowered citizen may be essential to an unfolding event. Which developments occur must follow established events, location, power constraints and NPC agency.

## F. Boundaries and unresolved worldbuilding

Still not established: nation, exact calendar year, worldwide map, politics, laws, languages, currencies, power taxonomy, biological rules, cosmic motives, or future factions. The **starting city, nearby places, three initial NPCs and transit institution** are now established in the fictional scenario; see the starting-cast and starting-locations records. These require owner choices or clearly documented reversible in-story establishment. Never copy another game's storyline.

When a player provides only minimal background, choose a **small, ordinary, reversible local scene** consistent with what they said. State material setting assumptions, avoid forcing a personal biography or irreversible character trait, and save details *only when established through narration and user input*.
