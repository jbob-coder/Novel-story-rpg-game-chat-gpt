# CH-000 — Narrator's preparation (not played history)

**Status:** Prepared scene framework. **No story events have occurred.** Do not treat sample dialogue, example NPCs or scene seeds here as canon or history.

## Player-facing dramatic purpose

At Day -30, the extraordinary has not yet arrived. Give the player a life worth caring about before future changes: an immediate task, one relationship, something they hope for, and one outside person pursuing a goal independently.

The chapter need **not** span the entire thirty days. Resolve naturally across short or long actions; advance in-world time only as justified. Preserve ordinary human choices, including the option to be uninterested in heroics.

## Minimum input

Ask the player for **who they are** and **where their ordinary day begins**. A name, broad age/background and location help but are not mandatory in a single response. If the player chooses to start without details, begin with a reversible anonymous everyday scene; avoid assigning irreversible facts like family composition, profession, income, powers or nationality.

Do not choose attributes or skill XP until background/training is established. Normal abilities and any specific day-to-day competence must be justified by the biography or play.

## Adaptive first-scene recipe

1. Choose a setting consistent with player input: home, school, job, street, travel, or another plausible place.
2. Introduce one **concrete obligation or opportunity** requiring an immediate decision, such as a delayed appointment, a request for help, or a conflicting commitment. These are *suggestions*, not fixed plot points.
3. If relevant, introduce one persistent supporting person and their independent short-term intention; record the new actor and what they know.
4. Describe 2–3 grounded details of the scene (weather, nearby sound, travel conditions, objects, clothing, speech) based on the established setting. Don't claim factual present-day weather without verification if a real date/place becomes material.
5. End at a natural player decision. Offer 2–3 examples only if useful and explicitly allow any action.
6. Upon action, log outcome, time and any relationship/skill change. Save event(s), chapter prose, state and finally manifest. Ensure the game can resume in a new chat without inferring anything from this preparation note.

## Optional scene seeds (not decided)

- **Neighborhood:** A planned errand is disrupted by someone else's urgent need. Helping, negotiating or leaving has later social consequences.
- **School:** A deadline and an interpersonal disagreement intersect; another student has their own plans beyond the protagonist.
- **Work:** An unexpected schedule change creates a choice involving money, safety, reputation or loyalty.
- **Travel:** A missed connection leads to a meeting or delayed obligation, with no supernatural foreknowledge.

These openings should differ based on the player background. Avoid universal templates pretending to describe the same life for everyone.

## Chapter opening quality gate

Before presenting the first actual scene, confirm:
- Player setup is either established or explicitly provisional.
- Time is Day -30, and no one has powers.
- At least one immediate contextually plausible action exists.
- NPC identities/goals are stable when introduced.
- Chapter events remain empty until actual player input and resolution.
- The next response after a resolved turn can be saved and later recovered.
